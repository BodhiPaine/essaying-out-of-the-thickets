%%What started as a bit of expounding on faith as an introduction to Buddhism has become about dependent liberation%% 

The more I research and contemplate *saddhā* ('faith' in Buddhism), the more I understand that an exposition on faith is also, functionally and fundamentally, an introduction to Buddhism, which necessarily starts as an introduction to the Buddha himself, and also his dhamma and the sangha he founded 2600 years ago. So I am seeing that this essay is, or needs to be, a guide to becoming a disciple of the Buddha. I hadn't really considered this to be possible until I migrated to the Theravada tradition, and now it is no longer optional, but rather, optimal compared with remaining dependent on dhamma teachers, ordained or otherwise. Because faith in Buddhism starts with *saddhā* in the Triple Gem, in the Buddha, the dhamma, and the sangha. I'll unpack that a bit more later. 

Meanwhile it is important to appreciate, as most (reasonable and confident) people do, that faith in Buddhism doesn't start with faith in gurus, and it doesn't start with faith in God, or in anything [[supernatural]]. It does, however, tend to start with the end of faith in other things we once held dear: hedonism, the government, [[metaphysical materialism]], literalism, science, technology. People, especially Westerners, come to Buddhism because we are suffering and nothing in our culture seems to have an authentic solution to that problem. We try seeking pleasure because we think it is the opposite of suffering, and therefore a pathway to happiness. Fail. When that doesn't work, we blame the government ~ we try to [[the Fourth Estate|keep them accountable on behalf of the people]], but fail. When that doesn't work, we start to wonder if there might be something broken in the fabric: the #human-condition is a curse and *we're all going to hell in a hand basket*. If one doesn't murder themselves at this point, it is because they ~~are fortunate enough~~ have the #kamma and the conditions for it to ripen in the form of beginning to suspect there is something more to life, something we are missing and if we can just find it, the magic, the joy, the peace, the wonder, and the awe. 

Until then it all just feels like a massive drag, at least for many of us ~ I did meet one Australian monk recently who said he had a pretty chill kind of life, no major suffering, just a deep sense that there was something else to do here. The call to ordain dropped on him suddenly, and he cried tears of relief. 

That feeling of relief is the voice of *saddhā*, of a deeply felt[^2] conviction that there is a way to be genuinely happy in this world, in this life ~ and maybe, just maybe, if we can drop our attachment to the conditioning we have been steeped in for [[the post-Enlightenment era|the last 400 years or so]] ... since, roughly, Descartes, perhaps, although I don't know why I always feel the tendency to blame him, specifically. There is a [[where in history the West started to go so horridly astray|whole massive branch]] to be explored on the subject of where in history the West started to go so horridly astray, and I'll get to that, don't you worry. Meanwhile, here we are in the twenty-first century wondering, *If science and rationalism and medicine and technology and 'representative democracy' are so great, why are there so many and various epidemics, so much disparity, so much insanity, so few authentic solutions to what is now being called the polycrisis?* The future we were promised has not been delivered ~ must have got stuck in the mail ~ and instead someone left the head of a dead horse on the doorstep. %%Fuck yeah, proud of that one!%%

In the midst of the despair that ensues in anyone who is only half-asleep, one eye perceiving the world as a quagmire of heinous bullshit most of the time, and the other clogged with conjunctivitis because even pollen is toxic now[^1], it really does come as a massive relief to come across the #dhamma. Finally, a community of people, thousands of years old and totalling ~5 billion, %%after waiting minutes for Claude to compile a complex response%% who have been wondering the same thing only wait ... that means this is not a modern problem after all. That right there alone is the sort of perspective that can pull a person out of the funk. 

This, I suppose, is why Buddha taught how suffering can be leveraged for the end of suffering ~ a paradox, I know, but paradoxes are just so damn cool, like a battle axe to the head of #literalism and #dualism and that devil of [[epistemology]]: #certainty.  He taught this in a discourse called the [Upanisa Sutta](https://suttacentral.net/sn12.23/en/sujato), and Bhikkhu Bodhi wrote [a brilliant essay about it](https://www.accesstoinsight.org/lib/authors/bodhi/wheel277.html) called 'Transcendental Dependent Arising: A Translation and Exposition of the Upanisa Sutta'. Dependent arising (or dependent origination) is a chain of causality the Buddha discovered that explains why we suffer ~ transcendental dependent arising then (or dependent liberation) is a set of instructions for how to transcend suffering.

What I mean by that is perhaps best expounded with reference to the sutta that first really brought me over to the idea that I had better get my faith on: the Upanisa Sutta.
