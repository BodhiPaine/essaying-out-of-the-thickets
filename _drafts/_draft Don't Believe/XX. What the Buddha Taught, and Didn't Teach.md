An important aspect of cultivating *saddhā*, hopefully obviously, is understanding what the Buddha taught, and didn't teach. As an exercise, I consider it worthwhile combing through the suttas and documenting them as part of considering whether I can feel confident about them. 

We can start with a simple but extraordinarily helpful teaching found in the somewhat famous MN 20, about how to deal with distracting thoughts. Can you imagine the thrill of #relief I felt upon learning there was a whole sutta dedicated to this?! And then! I later learnt there is something called [[#The Thought Trilogy]], with MN 18 and MN 19 both dealing with this subject. Thought Trilogy, I love it.

## MN 20
In this sutta, Buddha teaches five methods for dealing with distracting thoughts: 

- replace the unwholesome with the wholesome; if that doesn't work,
- examine the danger of unwholesome thoughts; if that doesn't work, 
- try to forget or ignore the thoughts ~ don't give them #attention; if that doesn't work, 
- still the thought formations; if that doesn't work, 
- crush mind with mind

Sujato points out that this single sutta alone dispels the myth that there is a single meditation method like a once-size-fits-all hard hat on the building site of reality. Of course, if you only have a hammer, everything looks like a nail ~ but everything is not a nail, and you don't only have a hammer. So you can think of these suttas as like Homebrew, which I recently learnt is a package of digital tools that can be downloaded to complement the existing tools on your computer. Except reality is not on your computer ~ at least I don't think it is, but who knows 🤷

Steps 1 and 2 are straightforward enough, but I'm right there with you if your brain's letterbox raised a little red flag around ignoring thoughts: a whole and significant [[sources of doubt | source of doubt]] for me has been the questions around whether such instructions might result in #repression if they are mis-understood or applied. So it's worth unpacking that a bit because I agree with Jung saying that the only way to enlightenment is *illuminating the unconscious*%%citation%%, not feeding it by stuffing yet more dark thoughts in there.
