This is a major [[edge]] for me - an attachment to certain #forms of #practice. I think it will help to lean-in to [[an organic practice|the Noble Eightfold Path]] to support a lifestyle of constant practice that doesn't require certain amount of cushion time or whatever.

For whatever reasons (kamma, upbringing, culture, lack of confidence) I have felt a strong need to maintain #discipline in the form of an external practice structure or schedule. My attachment has been leavened somewhat over the years, by learning that I am not the boss of reality, especially when I was co-piloting a small family but generally by the mill of living as a layperson - the household life is cramped and dusty. Even the culture in some monasteries is not conducive to meditation at all, less any formal structure I might imagine I need.

It also helped leaven my attachment to learn about *kammaṭṭhāna*[^1], meaning 'place of practice' or 'place of work', literally 'workplace'. Whatever our primary practice, *mulakammaṭṭhāna*[^2] is, we take it everywhere with the Path as our guide. 

And training in Zen helped - my teacher was big on the idea that the measure of our practice is our conduct. To become better humans (in [[this lifetime at least]]) we need to apply ourselves in [[each moment we remember]].

It seems obvious to say now, but I had been wading in the shallows around Tibetan Buddhism for so long and it wasn't until moving into Zen that I started seeing the importance of treating all activity as an opportunity for meditation.

[^1]: *kammaṭṭhāna*, work; activity; business; lit. 'workplace'

[^2]: *mūlakammaṭṭhāna*, primary meditation object; main meditation; lit. 'root workplace'
