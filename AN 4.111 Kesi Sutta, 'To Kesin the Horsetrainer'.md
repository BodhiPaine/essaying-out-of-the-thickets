We have Buddha talking with Kesi the horse trainer, who destroys any horse that doesn't respond to training. Kesi is a bit shocked when Buddha says he does the same: if a student doesn't respond to training, he 'kills' them or ends their training by withdrawing admonishment.

> It’s true, Kesi, it’s not appropriate for a Buddha to kill living creatures. But when a person in training doesn’t follow any of these forms of training, the Buddha doesn’t think they’re worth advising or instructing, and neither do their sensible spiritual companions. For it is killing in the training of the Noble One when the Buddha doesn’t think they’re worth advising or instructing, and neither do their sensible spiritual companions
