A competent cook pays attention to whether his master is satisfied with the dishes being served. In the same way, a competent meditator is aware of whether his practice is improving #concentration and reducing [[The Defilements|defilements]], or not. If not, then a meditator needs to choose another approach or object. Because if you only have a hammer, everything looks like a nail \[==or the other way round==?] 

But also, the simile of the cook teaches the need to know various meditation techniques to suitably nourish one's happiness and spiritual progress. In a similar vein, there were [[certain wholesome pleasures]] the Buddha encouraged us to allow because a long and successful holy life depends on being adequately nourished in meditation. #jhana

As the cook pays attention to his master's reactions, to know whether a practice is developing in the right fashion, a meditator must pay attention to the 'sign of the mind', and we do this by developing *[[07. What the Buddha Taught, and Didn't Teach#Wise Attention, Yoniso Manasikara|yoniso manasikara]]*, 'wise attention' or attention placed at the origin of experience where we can see reactions arise. 

I might have stated elsewhere how profoundly insightful this seems to me right now, this understanding of applying wise attention to *[[🎯 origin of experience|pure experience]]* - it seems that here we can get ahead of the eight ball, so to speak. If we can more consistently inhabit the space of pure experience, our suffering is bound to reduce.
