Perhaps my first encounter with Buddhist faith (*saddhā*) was at a Tibetan centre in Adelaide, inquiring about how to hold right view when I had not yet had direct experience of the truth myself – and being told I needed to take it on faith. This didn’t exactly inspire ~confidence~ in me at the time, but there are seven other Path factors to consider and so I focused on those. In fact, I think that is part of the advice I got: focus on the other Path factors, and faith in right view will naturally develop, which I do understand in ways I hope to cover in this essay. 

However, a wheel with a single untended spoke is bound to yield a bumpy ride at best, and so it was: not looking into right view because I thought it could wait until later in my development, I left untended a wide range of half-(un)conscious doubts that would rear their head from time to time. And by ‘wide’ I mean ‘cosmological’: is enlightenment in this lifetime within the range of my worldview? If not, then doubt. If so, then striving. But I'm getting ahead of myself.

It may be more pertinent to first point out the elephant in the room: ‘faith’ is almost a dirty word in the minds of many secular folk, especially modern Westerners like myself, and especially because it is tinged with religiosity. No sciencey person wants their credibility to be tainted by being perceived as faithful, where 'faith' is taken to mean 'belief without evidence'. In dhamma practice, faith has a different function (more like 'conviction' or 'confidence'), as denoted by the Pali, *saddhā*. The Buddha never asked us to commit the sort of 'blind belief' that makes our local half-(un)conscious metaphysical materialist relatively okay with bleeding from the anus due to unknown causes, but never from the eyes. Forget about statues bleeding from the eyes! Just a hallucination. What the Buddha did ask of us ~ nay, demanded, sometimes with a stern admonishment ~ is that we hear the dhamma, reflect upon the dhamma, apply the dhamma, and see for oneself whether dhamma is true, or not.

"But that was India and twenty-five-hundred years ago ~ keep up, lad!"

"Sorry Dad, we can go back to talking about the weather forecast if you like."

Because we place trust in so many things without even realising it and the weather forecast is just the beginning, because, not yet feeling sufficiently righteous about [[epistemology|the right way to know]], we[^1] proceed to scoff at others for having faith in the so-called supernatural or ('Heaven forbid', we say, apparently unaware of the ~irony~) religion, only because our scientism hasn't yet discovered that the super- becomes redundant upon direct experience of something like prophetic dreams or spontaneous astral travel during a near-death experience or Jesus/Mohammad/Gandalf at the end of the tunnel or ... skies raining frogs.

'Seeing is believing,' we say, because we haven't been with Buddhism for long enough as a culture to understand that the mind itself is also a [[sense faculty]], and one that can 'see' things the eyes cannot. 

[^1]: recovering materialists
