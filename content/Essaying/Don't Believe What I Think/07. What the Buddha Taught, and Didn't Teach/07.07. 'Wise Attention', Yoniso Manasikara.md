> [!NOTE] You get what you pay ~~for~~ *attention to*
> Buddha taught that *yoniso manasikara* (directing our attention at the origin of things) would lead inexorably toward awakening.

The Pali "yoni" means origin or womb, and I suppose being attentive to the origin of things comes along with knowing their arising, their temporary dwelling, then cessation. So that wise attention might be awareness ~~that~~ *as* every thing rises, dwells, then ceases. #impermanence #anicca 

One of the Hillside Hermitage monks, Anīgha%%link%% has it that *yoniso manasikara* and hearing the dhamma are two of factors of [[07.03. Full Awakening, or Liberation from Suffering 🎯#Stream-entry The first stage of awakening|stream entry]], for the arising of right view.%%add the other two where relevant%%

Another Hillside Hermitage monk, Thero %%link%% says the 'womb' is the background against which we foreground our meditation object but our attention should be diffuse, allowing one to not-observe[^3] that background or periphery as *the origin of our experience*, and that this is the practice of *yoniso manasikara*.

Anīgha's essay cites teachings that *yoniso manasikara* is a necessary cause for 'destruction of the taints', for ==[[#Full Awakening, or Liberation from Suffering 🎯|complete liberation]]==, and is itself a cause of 'great good', etc., and further, offers to helps \[emphasis added]

> come closer to what the Buddha meant with the term in a way that is consistent with its etymology and all its occurrences in the texts, and most importantly, tells us how and why it is the one thing responsible for the arising of those states conducive to freedom from suffering, and *how we can practically go about developing it it*.    

A teaching couldn't be more fundamental than this, so it's a very important idea and practice to understand, not misunderstand. 

Thero continues describing the practice, that by allowing an arisen experience to *be*, attention of the background/periphery manifests #indirectly, like falling asleep happens indirectly when one directs the mind away from entanglement with itself, toward an anchor in the body or its shifting sensations. Falling asleep results *indirectly* from efforts directed elsewise than sleeping.

I think I intuited this periphery-as-object practice already, taking up the space for experience as my *==kamatana==*, and perhaps it's just open awareness or ==shikantaza==(?) and *zazen*. I think of it as something like *reclining in the void* because one becomes relaxed when just inhabiting this space without taking up every suggestion of an object and not clinging too hard to a single object. It's not a state I can enter right away, but I know enough about its causes and conditions to have [[01. The Nature of Doubt#Self-efficacy|confidence]] that such an inhabitance is possible on a more consistent basis.

Such an inhabitance is alluded to by Frankl as well, in his idea of the ==space between== stimulus and response. 

There is a trend among contemporary meditation circles to teach one-pointed focus on the foreground object, but this practice precludes awareness of the background, of the origin of experience.

Should I say [[🎯 origin of experience|🎯 origin of our experience]] one more time? I will say again how thrilled I am to have gained some understanding of *yoniso manasikara* through this process. It happens that the practice of wise attention is a key feature of the path leading to the realisation of stream-entry, so that's cool!
