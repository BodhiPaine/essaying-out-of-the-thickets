A turning point for me in cultivating *saddhā* was when I started discovering that the Buddha variously taught about how to appraise both his teachings and the teachings of others - famously, in the [[Kalama Sutta]]. So we have *dhamma* and *adhamma*, that which is *not the dhamma*. We also have 'true dhamma' (saddhamma) and 'untrue dhamma' (asaddhamma). There are also some good reference points in [[MN 60 Apaṇṇaka Sutta, 'A Safe Bet'|MN 60]], Buddha's equivalent of [[Pascal’s Wager]]. In this sutta, the Buddha states explicitly that #disbelief in #rebirth is 'untrue dhamma', *asaddhamma*, and that such proponents are engaging in *asaddhammasaññatti*.

Important distinctions to make, obviously, ==but how==? How do we get to the heart of what the Buddha taught, and how can we discern whether our contemporary understanding of the mind and reality are  #dhamma-aligned, or not, which is to say, *dhamma* or *adhamma*, *saddhamma* or asaddhamma 'conterfeit' dhamma. 

An important part of understanding what constitutes *dhamma* and *adhamma* might also be framed as the questions, *[[07.02. Can Dhamma Save the World?|Can the dhamma save the world? 👉🏻]]* Of course I am not the only one to wonder about this, and there seems to be a whole sub-genre of suttas dedicated to one monk%%Uttiya?%% who was always asking about this too.
