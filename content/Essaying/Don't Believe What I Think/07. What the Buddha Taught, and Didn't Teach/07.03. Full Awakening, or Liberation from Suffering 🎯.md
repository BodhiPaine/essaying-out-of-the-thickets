The Buddha's teachings do have an ultimate direction, a single aim, with a [[06. Cultivating Saddhā#*Ekāyano Maggo*, The 'Direct Path'|path leading only in that direction]], and that is to *liberate oneself from suffering.* That is the #ultimate-destination, and to get there we're going to need ==[[#The Four Noble Truths|a map]]==, the Four Noble Truths. 

The objective of Buddhism is to eliminate the suffering caused by the Three Poisons, greed, aversion, and delusion, by training our mind to see reality clearly, and it is possible to achieve this in this lifetime ([[AN 3.55]]). We do this be strengthening our #concentration and using that clarity to investigate reality so that we cease conflating the impermanent with the permanent and suffering with happiness, by becoming awakened at the level of an *arahant*, who no longer filters reality through those ==[[#The Defilements|defilements]]== of greed, aversion, and delusions. 
 
It can’t be described beyond that, as far as I know - the best we can do is treat it like a reverse-negative image: we describe it by describing what it isn’t - that is, awakening is the absence of greed, aversion, and delusion. It is the absence of suffering, that is the objective of Buddhism.  

Buddha described a series of meaningful benchmarks along the Path from full-blown ignorant worldling through the four stages of awakening to fully awakened arahant, and learning to home in on what is required for [[#Stream-entry The first stage of awakening|the early stages]] has helped me to access a few small fruits from applying the method the Buddha described, which begets confidence.

Once a practitioner has faith arising from direct experience of the fruits of practice, they are empowered to embark on the Path with confidence, begetting energy, mindfulness, concentration, and wisdom - the five faculties. Without knowing where we're going, it can be hard to remain confident we can get there, so I find it helpful to check-in sometimes on the teaching that awakening is a real possibility, a genuine aspect of our human potential that is accessible to anyone who wants to make the [[00.01. Faith and Effort|effort]].

Once a practitioner cracks into the four stages of awakening, as a sotapanna it is said their faith becomes 'unshakeable' and they are destined ultimate awakening within the next seven years.

Reading from the Buddha such quantified statements around the subject and experience of awakening makes me a bit wary because elsewhere he talks about the ==[[07. What the Buddha Taught, and Didn't Teach#The Ten Unprofitable Questions|maddening futility]]== of trying to understand the internal mechanisms of kamma. But I remain inspired by the well-described and -trodden paths out of ignorance, especially after confirming recently I had progressed to higher stages of meditation without knowing the canonical term for the experience. 

These preliminary benchmarks on the progress bar of meditation practice are the early indicators one's practice is developing in the right way, and something worth contemplating if one is experiencing #doubt - reflecting on previous meditation progress, and on the [[teachings about the potential for awakening]]. %%find the kind of suttas that talk about having the same senses as the Buddha, every human has the potential for awakening%%

## *Dhammanusari* and *Saddhanusari*
One becomes a *dhammanusari* or *saddhanusari* by bringing the [[04. The Five Faculties|five faculties]] to an unspecified maturity, and by doing so is considered a sort of pre-noble noble one, or ==*ariyasāvaka*==. It is said that by achieving such a state it is guaranteed that one will become a stream-enterer at least at the time of death.
