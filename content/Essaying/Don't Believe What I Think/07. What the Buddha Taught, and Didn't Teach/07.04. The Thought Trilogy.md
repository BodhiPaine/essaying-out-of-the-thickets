The Buddha taught how to manage unruly thoughts.
# MN 18 Madhupiṇḍika Sutta, 'The Ball of Honey'
Re-reading this now and feeling swamped by how [[psychologically technical]] this sutta gets, especially regarding the subject of ==sanya==, and along with many others about variations of [[07. What the Buddha Taught, and Didn't Teach#Dependent Origination and Dependent Liberation||dependent origination]], I fall back on this sutta explicitly referencing that *the dhamma leads to freedom from conflict* even if I don't fully understand the mechanisms yet. (Cf. [[07.02. Can Dhamma Save the World?]]%%and mention there that MN 18 has a bit about ending conflict%%) 

It's one of the few suttas I know of that approaches how practice of the dhamma directly contributes to #social-justice, by ridding the individual of the inclination to engage in conflict, but I know there are others.
# MN 19 Dvedhāvitakka Sutta, 'Two Sorts of Thinking'

> [!NOTE] Inclining the mind
> 'Whatever a mendicant frequently thinks about and considers becomes their heart’s inclination' (Sujato)

in which two kinds of thinking are discussed

wholesome thinking, and 
unwholesome thinking

> I made thinking imbued with sensuality, thinking imbued with ill will, & thinking imbued with harmfulness one sort, and thinking imbued with renunciation, thinking imbued with non-ill will, & thinking imbued with harmlessness another sort.

# MN 20 Vitakkasaṇṭhāna Sutta, 'Working with Distraction'
In this sutta, Buddha teaches five methods for dealing with distracting thoughts: 

1. replace the unwholesome with the wholesome; if that doesn't work,
2. examine the danger of unwholesome thoughts; if that doesn't work, 
3. try to forget or ignore the thoughts ~ don't give them #attention; if that doesn't work, 
4. still the thought formations; if that doesn't work, 
5. crush mind with mind

Sujato points out that this single sutta alone dispels the myth that there is a single meditation method like a once-size-fits-all hard hat on the building site of reality. Of course, if you only have a hammer, everything looks like a nail ~ but everything is not a nail, and you don't only have a hammer. So you can think of these suttas as like Homebrew, which I recently learnt is a package of digital tools that can be downloaded to complement the existing tools on your computer. Except reality is not on your computer ~ at least I don't think it is, but who knows 🤷

Steps 1, 2 and 4 are straightforward enough, but I'm right there with you if your brain's letterbox raised a little red flag around 3 and 5: a whole and significant ==[[01. The Nature of Doubt#Sources of Doubt|source of doubt]]== for me has been the questions around whether such instructions might result in #repression if they are mis-understood or applied. So it's worth unpacking that a bit because I agree with Jung saying that the only way to enlightenment is *illuminating the unconscious*%%citation%%, not feeding it by stuffing yet more dark thoughts in there.
