## Buddhānusati
*contemplation of the Buddha*

By contemplating the Buddha we can renew our faith in our primary teacher, who for this purpose was said to have [[#The Nine Qualities of the Buddha|nine qualities]]. 
### Purpose

- inspire faith, offsetting the [[01. The Nature of Doubt#Doubt in the Five Hindrances|hindrance]] of #doubt 
- brighten & uplift the mind, arousing the [[04. The Five Faculties|faculty]] of #energy and potentially offsetting the the hindrance of #drowsiness 
- endeavouring to embody the Buddha's qualities
- promoting #renunciation, by considering how Buddha renounced the luxury of palace life and became an ascetic wanderer ~ easier to do in ancient India, but still inspiring

### The Nine Qualities of the Buddha
There are nine classical virtues of the Buddha, each of which 'expands upon and clarify' the three main categories (Khantipālo): wisdom, purity, compassion.

- *arahaṁ*, 'the accomplished destroyer of defilements'[^1]
- *sammā sambuddho*, 'a Buddha perfected by himself'
- *vijjā-caraṇa-sampanno*, 'complete in clear knowledge and compassionate conduct'
- *sugato*, 'supremely good in presence and in destiny'
- *lokavidu*, 'knower of the worlds'
- *anuttaro purisadamma-sārathī*, 'incomparable master of those to be tamed'
- *satthā deva-manussānaṁ*, 'teacher of devas and humanity'
- *buddho*, 'awakener and awakened'
- *bhagavā*, 'the lord of skilful means apportioning dhamma'

There may be nine classical virtues but there are overlaps and I am bound to parse each and all of them in variously shifting ways, so there is some value in making my own list to one day memorise in support of being able to call these to mind as required:

- self-awakened #SammāSambuddho
- taught the dust-free out of compassion
- valued #pragmatism and the #empiricism of the human senses (the fathom-long body)
- valued social justice
- valued inquiry and the power of the human intellect
- understood that awakening was a latent potential in all (human[^4]) beings, even if not each individual had the required disposition/capacity/kamma in this lifetime
- travelled widely, and for 45 years, to teach the Dhamma #sugato 

Some of these, such as "valued inquiry and the power of the human intellect" are going to derive from the qualities of the dhamma recorded for the purpose of *dhammānussati*.

#### Sammā Sambuddho
##### a Buddha perfected by himself
#wisdom 

The Buddha was self-awakened, not by following another's teaching, and truly a cosmological marvel[^2] because such a being appears only once the [[saddhamma, true dhamma]] has long vanished from the world. Khantipālo highlights that the Buddha was a regular human being who realised the truth by virtue of his own efforts: he was not bestowed special or arcane insight by a god, and 'owes his enlightenment to none'. The Buddha was not descended from some supernatural realm[^3] or being, and yet has transcended human limitations. We might not have the potential to become a buddha because that would defy the laws of cosmology, but we can become enlightened disciples, arahants, by adhering to the Buddha's instructions.

This is inspiring because we each have all the same six senses and [[04. The Five Faculties|five faculties]] as he had and therefore we have the same potential. It is from within this epithet that I can anchor myself to fuelling faith in the potential of the human mind when I am feeling drowsy/vague, or doubtful about my own capacity.

# Canonical Support for *Saddhā*
## Heavenly Destiny
🗂️ #unsupportive

In Soma Thera’s essay, 'Faith In The Buddha’s Teaching', there are so many rich ideas in just the first four pages, and then more all the way through, starting with a guarantee the faithful will go to heaven:

- one who has mere/'but' faith in and affection for the Buddha and his teachings, when they are correctly expounded, is destined for heaven (MI 142[^1]), ... 

Leading with this does not give much support to [[#Canonical Support for *Saddhā*]] because our conduct is the only determinant of the future we might expect, and belief matters only to the extent it motivates [[wholesome conduct]] #wholesome #conduct. There is something questionable about saying one with mere faith is "as it were taken by the hand and placed in heaven", considering a belief exists in some Christians that merely inviting Jesus into one's heart ensures they have a place in heaven, freedom from sin, etc.

He does say elsewhere in the essay that genuine faith - or faith based on genuine understanding - necessarily motivates wholesome conduct because one of those ideas is #kamma. Still though, **the heaven guarantee troubles me** because doesn't Buddha say elsewhere that we cannot know where kamma will lead, beyond good=good? *If someone does a lot of unwholesome stuff in their life and gains genuine faith right at the end of their life, are they still going to heaven?* And apart from the actions in this life: what if past-life (bad) kamma is yet to find the conditions for ripening? Couldn't even the faithful then still be reborn in hell? Or can it be assumed that if one has gained human rebirth then all their bad past-life kamma must have been burned?

These (kinds of) questions honestly do need some kind of resolution for me and as such the [[#Heavenly Destiny]] teaching doesn't sit well with me in terms of reasoned conviction.
