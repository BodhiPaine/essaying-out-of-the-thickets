Cultivating Saddhā#Sanghānusati|sanghanusati]] %% Humans are shady, compared to animals, which are 'obvious', easy to tame. Then Buddha says there are four kinds of people:

- one who mortifies themselves, 
- who mortifies others, 
- mortifies themselves and others, and 
- one who mortifies neither themselves nor others, who 

"lives without wishes in this very life, quenched, cooled, experiencing bliss, with self become divine" (Sujato).

One who mortifies themselves is a 'naked ascetic' who goes about 'licking their hands'. 

One who mortifies others is butcher or hunter, etc. 

One who mortifies themselves and others is a depraved priest or politician. 

One who neither mortifies themselves nor mortifies others is someone who hears the dhamma, gains faith in the Buddha, and decides to go forth as a monk of the sangha., embarking on a life purified and polished like the conch shell. 

As such, MN 51 may be support for the argument ordination is the [[07.03. Full Awakening, or Liberation from Suffering 🎯#Awakening as Ultimate Service| best way to do service]] by realising awakening for oneself and others, because the fourth person is one who ordains.
