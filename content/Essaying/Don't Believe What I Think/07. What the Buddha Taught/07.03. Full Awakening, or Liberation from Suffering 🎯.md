The Buddha's teachings do have an ultimate direction, a single aim, with a [[06. Cultivating Saddhā#*Ekāyano Maggo*, The 'Direct Path'|path leading only in that direction]], and that is to *liberate oneself from suffering.* That is the ultimate destination, and to get there we're going to need [[07.08. The Four Noble Truths|a map]], the Four Noble Truths. 

In [[MN 118 Ānāpānassatisutta, 'Mindfulness of Breathing'|MN 118]] and [[MN 10 Satipaṭṭhāna Sutta, 'The Establishment of Mindfulness'|MN 10]] we have teachings that are very clear about the |potential for awakening, as well as in the [[MN 95 Caṅkīsutta, 'With Caṅkī'|Caṅkī Sutta]]. It might get to the point where I just need to list them, because another is, appropriately titled, [[AN 3.55 Nibbutasutta, 'Nibbāna'|AN 3.55]] Nibbuta Sutta, 'Nibbāna'.

The objective of Buddhism is to eliminate the suffering caused by the Three Poisons, greed, aversion, and delusion, by training our mind to see reality clearly, and it is possible to achieve this in this lifetime ([[AN 3.55 Nibbutasutta, 'Nibbāna']]). We do this be strengthening our #concentration and using that clarity to investigate reality so that we cease conflating the impermanent with the permanent and suffering with happiness, by becoming awakened at the level of an *arahant*, who no longer filters reality through those [[07.05. The Three Poisons|defilements]] of greed, aversion, and delusions. 
 
It can’t be described beyond that, as far as I know - the best we can do is treat it like a reverse-negative image: we describe it by describing what it isn’t - that is, awakening is the absence of greed, aversion, and delusion. It is the absence of suffering, that is the objective of Buddhism.  

Buddha described a series of meaningful benchmarks along the Path from full-blown ignorant worldling through the four stages of awakening to fully awakened arahant, and learning to home in on what is required for [[#Stream-entry The first stage of awakening|the early stages]] has helped me to access a few small fruits from applying the method the Buddha described, which begets confidence.

Once a practitioner has faith arising from direct experience of the fruits of practice, they are empowered to embark on the Path with confidence, begetting energy, mindfulness, concentration, and wisdom - the five faculties. Without knowing where we're going, it can be hard to remain confident we can get there, so I find it helpful to check-in sometimes on the teaching that awakening is a real possibility, a genuine aspect of our human potential that is accessible to anyone who wants to make the [[00.01. Faith and Effort|effort]].

Once a practitioner cracks into the four stages of awakening, as a [[07.03. Full Awakening, or Liberation from Suffering 🎯#Stream-entry The first stage of awakening|sotāpanna]] it is said their faith becomes 'unshakeable' and they are destined ultimate awakening within the next seven years.

Reading from the Buddha such quantified statements around the subject and experience of awakening makes me a bit wary because elsewhere he talks about the [[08.02. Metaphysics#The Ten Unprofitable Questions|maddening futility]] of trying to understand the internal mechanisms of kamma. But I remain inspired by the well-described and -trodden paths out of ignorance, and by low-hanging fruit on the path of meditation.

Faith is a natural precursor for the path of stream-entry, as understood from the teachings about *dhammanusari* or *saddhanusari*, a 'wisdom follower' and 'faith follower' respectively.  
## *Dhammanusari* and *Saddhanusari*
One becomes a *dhammanusari* or *saddhanusari* by bringing the [[04. The Five Faculties|five faculties]] to an unspecified maturity, and by doing so is called 'one who has entered the path to stream-entry', which I might call stream-aspirant for short. A stream-aspirant is no longer technically a *puthujana*, but also net yet a stream-winner, not yet an *ariyasāvaka*.%%add these to glossary%% It is said that by achieving such a state it is guaranteed that one will become a stream-enterer at least at the time of death.

Learning about the *dhammanusari* or *saddhanusari* was a turning point for me and, along with the Uttiya Sutta, the main reason I have been looking into faith ~ however, faith is just one of the faculties that need to cultivated or matured. Mindfulness and concentration are also essential, and the Buddha offers a few seminal suttas for [[07.04. The Thought Trilogy|working with thoughts 👉🏻]] to support concentration especially.

Once I understood there is no way of discerning whether these faculties are sufficiently matured, I began to understand they are a path, the same as *the* Path, and as such must be co-developed. As a path, developing the faculties is called *sotapatti-maggattha*: “the path to stream-entry” So here we have another fork.
