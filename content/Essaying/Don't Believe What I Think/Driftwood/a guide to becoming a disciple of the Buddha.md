Me being your guide in this sense is like if we met one of my friends at the park: all I could do is introduce you, and then the conversation is up to you. And it *is* a conversation that we need to have with the Buddha (through the dhamma, with support from the sangha), in the sense we need to ask questions and seek elaboration on points we don't yet understand.

We've all met someone who doesn't do this ~ they either talk about themselves the whole time, or they drift off like they're planning to be anywhere else. Don't be that person. 

We may need to come back to the same teaching again and again before some understanding or insight drops into place and makes our [[epistemology|knowledge more embodied, more felt than known]], really, more a part of psychophysical makeup so that our actions flow naturally from this new #awareness instead of from our old #conditioning.

So although I am explaining various concepts, I will endeavour to provide as much of the original source material as possible, either in citations or as longer [[Sutta Readings]], so the reader can go and check for themselves whether they agree with what I am saying about what I think Buddha was saying (see the [[_Caveat]] about that). 

I don't want to teach you Buddhism; what I am doing here is more about articulating my own understanding, as a process of writing- and learning-in-public. If you happen to gain some understanding from that then honestly, that is secondary. My research into *saddhā* began in earnest around two things: learning about the canonical concept of the *dhammanusari* and *saddhanusari*; and learning about the Upanisa Sutta. Faith really is a central tenet of Buddhism, no matter what others tell you about being able to practice Buddhism *without belief*. How can we *practice anything*, without belief?

So there will be an extensive section on how to become accustomed to reading the suttas but also, because the Buddha is no longer living there is an extensive section about finding guidance ~ which starts with the suttas but also depends on teachers and wise friends, kalyana-mitta. I mean, ultimately the guidance comes from the #Dhamma-Vinaya but we also need those with ideally a lot more experience (teacher-practitioners) to help us navigate the teachings, and we need peers (student-practitioners) on the Path, meaning those whose level of development is closer to ours. So we might say 'dhamma instructors' rather than 'teachers', and these kinds of peers are called *kalyana-mitta*, meaning 'beautiful' or 'wise' associates. 

The Buddha himself taught us how we should appraise a teacher/instructor (and a *kalyana-mitta*), as well as their teachings, and in this chapter we may look at some contemporary teachings to see if they align with both the dhamma itself, as well as the Buddha's instructions on how to choose from the instructions of other teachers. 

I say this because I have these transcripts from Thanissaro's retreats on the theme of the Five Spiritual Faculties, and sometimes I have my doubts about Than because I have heard he has a temper. I wonder, *Can I trust the instructions of a teacher who yells at his drivers?* So I want to assess whether his teachings are dhamma, or not, because whatever they are, they resonate with something in me and so I want to accept them. This *wanting* to accept them, like Mulder wanting to believe, is an act of *saddhā*: there is a desire to believe a teaching because there is need for liberation, but neither this desire nor the need are sufficient reason to believe, of course ~ such desire is just the beginning of *saddhā*; the next step is #investigation.

Contemporary teachers often speak our language, and they provide their understanding of the dhamma in a narrative form that is often absent from the suttas. This means we can relate to them more directly than we can to the suttas because they speak to experiences we are familiar with, but also they elaborate on concepts that it seems are only alluded to in the suttas. But how do we know these elaborations are (aligned with the) dhamma and are not leading us astray[^1] by resonating with something like wrong view in us? We may resonate or like the contemporary teaching because it validates some misperception or other egoic attachment to wrong understanding.

[^1]: precisely when we have decided that we want to stop allowing ourselves to be lead astray, by #hedonism and the like
