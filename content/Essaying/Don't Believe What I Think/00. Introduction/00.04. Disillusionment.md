👈🏼 previous note, [[00.03. Fallbacks]]

No matter how shaky my *saddhā* might be, my faith in hedonistic materialism is shakier. I have been disillusioned with regular materialism as well as [[06. Cultivating Saddhā#Philosophy of Science|metaphysical materialism]] for a long time, not to mention hedonism. 

Reading [[The Philosopher's Secret Fire|Harpur]] and other forays into subjects like [[06. Cultivating Saddhā#Philosophy of Science|philosophy of science]] has helped me to understand that materialism and post-Enlightenment rational #literalism are a dead-end, having stripped the world of half its reality - leaving us dangling in a perception of spacetime arbitrated by the 'scientific priesthood', with nowhere to go but the grave, or an eternal afterlife that is not guaranteed to be pleasant. #scientism #scientific-priesthood

I have been looking for an alternative for a long time, and have known that Buddhism offered one. I suppose the decision to ordain kicked things up a notch as well, and the general dhamma study I was doing already might have triggered a Dunning-Kruger effect: the more I learn, the more I know I need to learn. And it seemed the further I looked into the suttas the more I saw that my inherited worldview might be subtly [[00.01. Faith and Effort|undermining my effort]] in practice. 

Essaying about this all has left me confident I have enough *saddhā* to keep my practice afloat for a good while - and not just [[mundane saddhā]], but saddha in [[07. What the Buddha Taught, and Didn't Teach#Full Awakening, or Liberation from Suffering 🎯|full liberation]].

There is nothing #liberative, that I know of, about the dominant worldview of modern Western cultures: if there were, we wouldn't be neurotically ferreting our way around the planet, eating everything we hope will displace, even just for a few moments, the discomfort of being unsure and afraid and [[human]]; the sort of rampant #consumerism we have somehow normalised wouldn't get a foothold in a culture whose dominant worldview included a means of (a [[psychological technology]] for) training one's mind and heart to be liberated from suffering.

I have solid faith that Buddhism does provide this means to actualise the human potential to [[Essential Suttas#SN 12.23 The Upanisa Sutta, 'Proximate Cause'|transcend suffering]], but understanding what phrases like 'transcend suffering' actually mean according to a Buddhist worldview is an essay that will keep on giving. A starting point for that, is the [Upanisa Sutta](https://suttacentral.net/sn12.23/en/sujato), about which Bhikkhu Bodhi wrote [a brilliant essay](https://www.accesstoinsight.org/lib/authors/bodhi/wheel277.html) called 'Transcendental Dependent Arising'. 

In this discourse the Buddha presents a classic formula of dependent arising (or [[07. What the Buddha Taught, and Didn't Teach#Dependent Origination and Dependent Liberation|dependent origination]]) as a chain of causality for suffering ~ followed by a formula of *transcendental* dependent arising (or [[08. Dependent Liberation]]), which is *a set of instructions* for *how to transcend suffering*.

That is, I sincerely believe that the Buddha's #dhamma presents a robust [[psychological technology|psychospiritual methodology]] for training the mind to see reality clearly so that we don't repeatedly suffer the mistake of conflating the mirage with the oasis. 

Hedonism, on the other hand, is failing us, clearly 👈🏼 *not* liberative.

==Eudaimonia== is an upgrade, but still not the whole package: flourishing is a legitimate option, now that [[transpersonal psychology]] has hit the scene. But even the most cutting-edge new-paradigm psychological modalities of the West do not cut the mustard (seed) when compared with the potential that Buddhism offers. 

I know I am starting to sound like a zealot now, and I am okay with that in the same way science zealots are okay with being that: it's a matter of conviction, of faith, of *saddhā* (which has not been monopolised by Buddhism: *saddhā* just happens to be the same idea[^4] expressed in Pali).
