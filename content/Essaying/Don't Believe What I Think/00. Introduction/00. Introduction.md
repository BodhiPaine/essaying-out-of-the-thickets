**Don't Believe What I Think:**
Essaying on having *saddhā* in faith

*Investing in mental development, rather than in* 
*accumulating wealth, means the chances are I will die poor* 
*and happy - whereas pursuing material wealth* 
*at the expense of mental development*
*risks dying poor anyway,* 
*but unhappy to boot.*

Perhaps my first encounter with Buddhist faith (*saddhā*) was at a Tibetan centre in Adelaide, inquiring about how to hold [[07. What the Buddha Taught, and Didn't Teach#Skilful View,* sammā-diṭṭhi*|skilful view]] when I had not yet had direct experience of the truth myself – and being told I needed to take it on faith. This didn’t exactly inspire ~confidence~ in me at the time, but there are seven other [[07. What the Buddha Taught, and Didn't Teach#the Eightfold Path|Path factors]] to consider and so I focused on those. In fact, I think that is part of the advice I got: focus on the other Path factors, and faith in skilful view will naturally develop.

However, a wheel with a single untended spoke is bound to yield a bumpy ride at best, and so it was: not looking into right view because I thought it could wait until later in my development, I left untended a wide range of half-(un)conscious [[01. The Nature of Doubt|doubts]] that would rear their head from time to time. And by ‘wide’ I mean ‘cosmological’: is [[07. What the Buddha Taught, and Didn't Teach#Full Awakening, or Liberation from Suffering 🎯|enlightenment]] in this lifetime within the range of my worldview? If not, then doubt. If so, then striving. But I'm getting ahead of myself. #cosmology 

My interest was aroused again when I was at Bodhivana[^7], but I see now that faith in a single authoritative teacher is different from the *saddhā* I have come to understand through this [[essaying|essaying]]. It was at Bodhivana that I found Bhikkhu Bodhi's essay about [[08. Dependent Liberation]], and it was faith again that I got stuck on. 

When I was at Dhammagiri after Bodhivana, I started hearing about the idea of the *dhammanusari* and *saddhanusari*, the wisdom- or faith-follower - one who has fully developed the [[04. The Five Faculties|the five faculties]]. It was said that such a practitioner was destined at least for [[stream-entry]] at the time of death - and, fancifully, that a world-system could not end while one remained a *dhammanusari* or *saddhanusari*. As well as these guarantees, it is in the suttas %%somewhere%% that developing the faculties to this extent was considered by the Buddha to be a kind of pre-noble noble one, or or ==*ariyasāvaka*==.

I love it when I find new stones on the Path, and ones that might even be within reach. So I was intrigued, and this was perhaps the tipping point, after which I could hardly prevent myself from looking more into faith. Starting with [[04. The Five Faculties|the five faculties]], because they are the faculties one must to develop to become a wisdom- or faith-follower.

The same as I wasn't keen on faith at the Tibetan centre in Adelaide, I knew that I would need to do some work on cultivating *saddhā*: if developing the faculties was a prerequisite for entering the stages of awakening and the *dhammanusari* and *saddhanusari* were preliminary to that, I needed to know more about what faith actually means in Buddhism, and what it doesn't mean.

My understanding of faith began to feel increasingly pressing as my study developed, because I began to see how compromised faith was impacting the [[#Faith and Effort|amount and type of effort👇🏼]] that I was applying.

# Faith and Effort
%%move to new note%%
The classic example is about going to the park: one would not set out if they weren’t convinced they could make it and that doing so would be worthwhile. 

If I don't believe it is possible for a person to become [[07. What the Buddha Taught, and Didn't Teach#Full Awakening, or Liberation from Suffering 🎯|awakened]] in this lifetime, or that such awakening will benefit others, I might 'hedge my bets', splitting my time between *bhāvanā* and social work - or between *bhāvanā* and writing a book, or endlessly fiddling with this garden essay. I am more likely to try helping in ways that are 'more direct', not in ways reliant upon obscure [[saving the world|metaphysical mechanisms]] one doesn't yet understand (cf. [[AN 10.95, Uttiya Sutta, ‘With Uttiya’|AN 10.95]], Uttiya Sutta).

Depending on my worldview, it sometimes comes up that achieving a literary goal (or even just the process of writing) will make me happy, or that I have only this life, so I need to make a contribution before I go. As much as I can say honestly I don't think the former will genuinely make me happy, or that the latter is true - I can know this objectively, but sometimes the thoughts come up in meditation, so having a place to work through reflections on that #conditioning will be helpful (to me).

As well as being concerned about how compromised faith was impacting my effort, there were other doubts that would surface now and then, and which I wanted to essay my through - so the essaying has been an exercise in [[#Surfacing Doubts|surfacing those doubts 👇🏼]] to have a better look at them.

# Surfacing Doubts
The first that comes to mind is whether [[07. What the Buddha Taught, and Didn't Teach#Full Awakening, or Liberation from Suffering 🎯|awakening is possible ✅]], and I am going to give them each a little badge as I go, because this is my working manual more than anything. I choose to believe this, in a form of [[Pascal’s Wager]] that feels a lot more like the saying, 'Aim for the stars - if you fail, you will still land on the moon.'

I worried sometimes that my inherited worldview was hindering my practice, but it turns out my worldview is a lot more Buddhist than I thought - the only major hurdle is the idea of 'progress' or [[linear history]], and the fantasy-consolation of humans evolving consciousness to an increasingly refined state. 

I was worried about feeling or being perceived as credulous, but I understand that *saddhā* is like water to the oil of credulity. As [[01. The Nature of Doubt#^soma-credulity|Soma says]], 'Study of a teaching through inquiring into it is unfavourable to credulity.'

One of the main sources of doubt for me over the last few years, since moving from the Mahayana tradition to the Theravada, is [[01. The Nature of Doubt#The Bodhisattva Ideal|the bodhisattva ideal]] and how this dove-tails so nicely with my [[01. The Nature of Doubt#I Should be 'Helping'|perceived need to save the world]]: a bodhisattva is the 'bleeding-hearted liberal' *par excellence*, the ultimate social-justice warrior. These are terms I do not use disparagingly - they are self-descriptors. 

There is a convincing case though anyway, that the idea of a multiple-lifetime 'bodhisattva career' is an interpretation of Buddhist cosmology that is not found in Early Buddhism - it was *added* to the cosmology later. 

People are putting 'Bodhisattva' on their business cards while in the cosmology, such an aspirant needs to make the vow in the presence of living buddha, who then has to prophecy whether the aspirant will experience the right conditions. So it's not going to happen until Matreya (the next buddha, not the hippy festival), and then it will be for one buddha only - there can be only one budda per world-system. 




Having articulated now this litany of surfaced doubts that were holding me back in practice, it is a heartfelt segue to something I realised only later in the essaying process: a large part of my motivation to pursue Buddhist practice is not so much faith in Buddhism, but from a long-time [[#Disillusionment|crisis of faith 👇🏼]] in my own inherited worldview.

It's kind of like the parable of the trapeze: having let go of any faith I might have once had in the mainstream worldview, I am sailing through the air in search of something to grasp about Buddhism.

# Disillusionment
I have been disillusioned with regular materialism as well as [[06. Cultivating Saddhā#Philosophy of Science|metaphysical materialism]] for a long time, not to mention hedonism. 

Reading [[The Philosopher's Secret Fire|Harpur]] and other forays into subjects like [[06. Cultivating Saddhā#Philosophy of Science|philosophy of science]] has helped me to understand that materialism and post-Enlightenment rational #literalism are a dead-end, having stripped the world of half its reality - leaving us dangling in a perception of spacetime arbitrated by the 'scientific priesthood', with nowhere to go but the grave, or an eternal afterlife that is not guaranteed to be pleasant. #scientism #scientific-priesthood

I have been looking for an alternative for a long time, and have known that Buddhism offered one. I suppose the decision to ordain kicked things up a notch as well, and the general dhamma study I was doing already might have triggered a Dunning-Kruger effect: the more I learn, the more I know I need to learn. And it seemed the further I looked into the suttas the more I saw that my inherited worldview might be subtly [[#Faith and Effort|undermining my effort]] in practice. 

Essaying about this all has left me confident I have enough *saddhā* to keep my practice afloat for a good while - and not just [[mundane saddhā]], but saddha in [[07. What the Buddha Taught, and Didn't Teach#Full Awakening, or Liberation from Suffering 🎯|full liberation]].

There is nothing #liberative, that I know of, about the dominant worldview of modern Western cultures: if there were, we wouldn't be neurotically ferreting our way around the planet, eating everything we hope will displace, even just for a few moments, the discomfort of being unsure and afraid and [[human]]; the sort of rampant #consumerism we have somehow normalised wouldn't get a foothold in a culture whose dominant worldview included a means of (a [[psychological technology]] for) training one's mind and heart to be liberated from suffering.

I have solid faith that Buddhism does provide this means to actualise the human potential to [[Essential Suttas#SN 12.23 The Upanisa Sutta, 'Proximate Cause'|transcend suffering]], but understanding what phrases like 'transcend suffering' actually mean according to a Buddhist worldview is an essay that will keep on giving. A starting point for that, is the [Upanisa Sutta](https://suttacentral.net/sn12.23/en/sujato), about which Bhikkhu Bodhi wrote [a brilliant essay](https://www.accesstoinsight.org/lib/authors/bodhi/wheel277.html) called 'Transcendental Dependent Arising'. 

In this discourse the Buddha presents a classic formula of dependent arising (or [[07. What the Buddha Taught, and Didn't Teach#Dependent Origination and Dependent Liberation|dependent origination]]) as a chain of causality for suffering ~ followed by a formula of *transcendental* dependent arising (or [[08. Dependent Liberation]]), which is *a set of instructions* for *how to transcend suffering*.

That is, I sincerely believe that the Buddha's #dhamma presents a robust [[psychological technology|psychospiritual methodology]] for training the mind to see reality clearly so that we don't repeatedly suffer the mistake of conflating the mirage with the oasis. 

Hedonism, on the other hand, is failing us, clearly 👈🏼 *not* liberative.

==Eudaimonia== is an upgrade, but still not the whole package: flourishing is a legitimate option, now that [[transpersonal psychology]] has hit the scene. But even the most cutting-edge new-paradigm psychological modalities of the West do not cut the mustard (seed) when compared with the potential that Buddhism offers. 

I know I am starting to sound like a zealot now, and I am okay with that in the same way science zealots are okay with being that: it's a matter of conviction, of faith, of *saddhā* (which has not been monopolised by Buddhism: *saddhā* just happens to be the same idea[^4] expressed in Pali). 

In the context of %%all this%%, I decided to see what I could learn by essaying my notes on *saddhā* as exercise in cultivating faith, *saddhābhāvanā*. Read that next, [[01. An Exercise in Saddhā]] 👉🏻, or follow any of the in-text links to explore the garden.
