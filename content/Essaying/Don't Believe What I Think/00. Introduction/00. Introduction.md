**Don't Believe What I Think:**
Essaying on having *saddhā* in faith

*Investing in mental development, rather than in* 
*accumulating wealth, means the chances are I will die poor* 
*and happy - whereas pursuing material wealth* 
*at the expense of mental development*
*risks dying poor anyway,* 
*but unhappy to boot.*

Perhaps my first encounter with Buddhist faith (*saddhā*) was at a Tibetan centre in Adelaide, inquiring about how to hold [[right view]] when I had not yet had direct experience of the truth myself – and being told I needed to take it on faith. This didn’t exactly inspire ~confidence~ in me at the time, but there are seven other [[Path factors]] to consider and so I focused on those. In fact, I think that is part of the advice I got: focus on the other Path factors, and faith in skilful view will naturally develop, which I do understand in ways ==I hope to cover in this essay==.%%how faith naturally develops from applying other factors%% 

However, a wheel with a single untended spoke is bound to yield a bumpy ride at best, and so it was: not looking into right view because I thought it could wait until later in my development, I left untended a wide range of half-(un)conscious [[01. The Nature of Doubt|doubts]] that would rear their head from time to time. And by ‘wide’ I mean ‘cosmological’: is [[07. What the Buddha Taught, and Didn't Teach#Full Awakening, or Liberation from Suffering 🎯|enlightenment]] in this lifetime within the range of my worldview? If not, then doubt. If so, then striving. But I'm getting ahead of myself. #cosmology 

My interest was aroused again when I was at Bodhivana, which is interesting when I come to think of it because the faith-heavy culture there is part of what deterred me from ordaining there - but I see now that faith in a single authoritative kruba-ajahn%%define%% is different from the *saddhā* I have come to understand through this [[essaying|essaying]]. It was also at Bodhivana that I found Bhikkhu Bodhi's essay about [[08. Dependent Liberation]], and it was faith again that I got stuck on.  %%bit about dhamma- and saddhanusari at Dhammagiri, and the conversation with Adicco about assuming the devas are watching%%

%%elaborate%%Learning about *dhammanusari* and *saddhanusari* was perhaps the tipping point, after which I could hardly prevent myself from looking more into faith, starting with [[04. The Five Faculties|the five faculties]], because they are the faculties one must to develop to become a wisdom- or faith-follower, and I think it was in this context that I saw how compromised faith was impacting the amount and direction of #effort that I was applying. 
# Faith and Effort
The classic example is about going to the park: one would not set out if they weren’t convinced they could make it and that doing so would be worthwhile. 

If I don't believe it is possible for a person to become [[07. What the Buddha Taught, and Didn't Teach#Full Awakening, or Liberation from Suffering 🎯|awakened]] in this lifetime, or that such awakening will benefit others, I might 'hedge my bets', splitting my time between *bhāvanā* and social work - or between *bhāvanā* and writing a book, or endlessly fiddling with this garden essay. I am more likely to try helping in ways that are 'more direct', not in ways reliant upon obscure [[saving the world|metaphysical mechanisms]] one doesn't yet understand (cf. [[AN 10.95, Uttiya Sutta, ‘With Uttiya’|AN 10.95]], Uttiya Sutta).

Depending on my worldview, it sometimes comes up that achieving a literary goal (or even just the process of writing) will make me happy, or that I have only this life, so I need to make a contribution before I do. As much as I can say honestly I don't think the former will genuinely make me happy, or that the latter is true - I can know this objectively, but sometimes the thoughts come up in meditation, so having a place to work through reflections on that #conditioning will be helpful (to me)

# Disillusionment
As well as being concerned about how compromised faith was impacting my effort, essaying on *saddhā* has been motivated by a crisis of faith in my own inherited worldview: I have been disillusioned with regular materialism as well as [[06. Cultivating Saddhā#Philosophy of Science|metaphysical materialism]] for a long time, not to mention hedonism. 

Reading [[The Philosopher's Secret Fire|Harpur]] and other forays into subjects like [[06. Cultivating Saddhā#Philosophy of Science|philosophy of science]] has helped me to understand that materialism and post-Enlightenment rational #literalism are a dead-end, having stripped the world of half its reality - leaving us dangling in a perception of spacetime arbitrated by the 'scientific priesthood', with nowhere to go but the grave, or an eternal afterlife that is not guaranteed to be pleasant. #scientism #scientific-priesthood

I have been looking for an alternative for a long time, and have known that Buddhism offered one. I suppose the decision to ordain kicked things up a notch as well, and the general dhamma study I was doing already might have triggered a Dunning-Kruger effect: the more I learn, the more I know I need to learn. 

And I felt faith was really holding me back, so why not have a crack at that? 

Essaying about this all has left me confident I have enough *saddhā* to keep my practice afloat for a good while - and not just [[mundane saddhā]], but saddha in [[07. What the Buddha Taught, and Didn't Teach#Full Awakening, or Liberation from Suffering 🎯|full liberation]].

There is nothing #liberative, that I know of, about the dominant worldview of modern Western cultures: if there were, we wouldn't be neurotically ferreting our way around the planet, eating everything we hope will displace, even just for a few moments, the discomfort of being unsure and afraid and [[human]]; the sort of rampant #consumerism we have somehow normalised wouldn't get a foothold in a culture whose dominant worldview included a means of (a [[psychological technology]] for) training one's mind and heart to be liberated from suffering.

I have solid faith that Buddhism does provide this means to actualise the human potential to [[Essential Suttas#SN 12.23 The Upanisa Sutta, 'Proximate Cause'|transcend suffering]], but understanding what phrases like 'transcend suffering' actually mean according to a Buddhist worldview is an essay that will keep on giving. A starting point for that, is the [Upanisa Sutta](https://suttacentral.net/sn12.23/en/sujato), about which Bhikkhu Bodhi wrote [a brilliant essay](https://www.accesstoinsight.org/lib/authors/bodhi/wheel277.html) called 'Transcendental Dependent Arising'. 

In this discourse the Buddha presents a classic formula of dependent arising (or [[07. What the Buddha Taught, and Didn't Teach#Dependent Origination and Dependent Liberation|dependent origination]]) as a chain of causality for suffering ~ followed by a formula of *transcendental* dependent arising (or [[08. Dependent Liberation]]), which is *a set of instructions* for *how to transcend suffering*.

That is, I sincerely believe that the Buddha's #dhamma presents a robust [[psychological technology|psychospiritual methodology]] for training the mind to see reality clearly so that we don't repeatedly suffer the mistake of conflating the mirage with the oasis. 

Hedonism, on the other hand, is failing us, clearly 👈🏼 *not* liberative.

==Eudaimonia== is an upgrade, but still not the whole package: flourishing is a legitimate option, now that [[transpersonal psychology]] has hit the scene. But even the most cutting-edge new-paradigm psychological modalities of the West do not cut the mustard (seed) when compared with the potential that Buddhism offers. 

I know I am starting to sound like a zealot now, and I am okay with that in the same way science zealots are okay with being that: it's a matter of conviction, of faith, of *saddhā* (which has not been monopolised by Buddhism: *saddhā* just happens to be the same idea[^4] expressed in Pali). To articulate why I feel this conviction, I think it is time, now, to actually pivot ~ a full 180, to the other side of the coin: [[01. The Nature of Doubt|the nature of doubt]].
