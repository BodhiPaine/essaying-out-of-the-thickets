**Don't Believe What I Think:**
Essaying on having *saddhā* in faith

*Investing in mental development, rather than in* 
*accumulating wealth, means the chances are I will die poor* 
*and happy - whereas pursuing material wealth* 
*at the expense of mental development*
*risks dying poor anyway,* 
*but unhappy to boot.*

Perhaps my first encounter with Buddhist faith (*saddhā*) was at a Tibetan centre in Adelaide, inquiring about how to hold [[07. What the Buddha Taught, and Didn't Teach#Skilful View,* sammā-diṭṭhi*|skilful view]] when I had not yet had direct experience of the truth myself – and being told I needed to take it on faith. This didn’t exactly inspire ~confidence~ in me at the time, but there are seven other [[07. What the Buddha Taught, and Didn't Teach#the Eightfold Path|Path factors]] to consider and so I focused on those. In fact, I think that is part of the advice I got: focus on the other Path factors, and faith in skilful view will naturally develop.

However, a wheel with a single untended spoke is bound to yield a bumpy ride at best, and so it was: not looking into right view because I thought it could wait until later in my development, I left untended a wide range of half-(un)conscious [[01. The Nature of Doubt|doubts]] that would rear their head from time to time. And by ‘wide’ I mean ‘cosmological’: is [[07. What the Buddha Taught, and Didn't Teach#Full Awakening, or Liberation from Suffering 🎯|enlightenment]] in this lifetime within the range of my worldview? If not, then doubt. If so, then striving. But I'm getting ahead of myself. #cosmology 

My interest was aroused again when I was at Bodhivana[^7], but I see now that faith in a single authoritative teacher is different from the *saddhā* I have come to understand through this [[essaying|essaying]]. It was at Bodhivana that I found Bhikkhu Bodhi's essay about [[08. Dependent Liberation]], and it was faith again that I got stuck on. 

When I was at Dhammagiri after Bodhivana, I started hearing about the idea of the *dhammanusari* and *saddhanusari*, the wisdom- or faith-follower - one who has fully developed the [[04. The Five Faculties|the five faculties]]. It was said that such a practitioner was destined at least for [[stream-entry]] at the time of death - and, fancifully, that a world-system could not end while one remained a *dhammanusari* or *saddhanusari*. As well as these guarantees, it is in the suttas %%somewhere%% that developing the faculties to this extent was considered by the Buddha to be a kind of pre-noble noble one, or or ==*ariyasāvaka*==.

I love it when I find new stones on the Path, and ones that might even be within reach. So I was intrigued, and this was perhaps the tipping point, after which I could hardly prevent myself from looking more into faith. Starting with [[04. The Five Faculties|the five faculties]], because they are the faculties one must to develop to become a wisdom- or faith-follower.

The same as I wasn't keen on faith at the Tibetan centre in Adelaide, I knew that I would need to do some work on cultivating *saddhā*: if developing the faculties was a prerequisite for entering the stages of awakening and the *dhammanusari* and *saddhanusari* were preliminary to that, I needed to know more about what faith actually means in Buddhism, and what it doesn't mean.

So if _saddhābhāvanā_ is a genuine Pali word, I can say this garden is an exercise in _saddhābhāvanā_ - in cultivating faith. I am building the essay for this garden because even though my _saddhā_ is generally sufficiently robust, I understand it may wane at any time[^9], so having a resource such as this - and having gone through such a process to build it - will come in handy.

My understanding of faith began to feel increasingly pressing as my study developed, because I began to see how compromised faith was impacting the [[00.01. Faith and Effort|amount and type of effort 👉🏻]] that I was applying.
