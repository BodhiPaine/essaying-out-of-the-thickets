In rather unclear academic ESL, he tries to make the case that #delusion (as understood in Buddhism) is the cause of the ecological crisis, which is something I obviously sympathise with but unfortunately I would say he doesn’t support the cause because his argument is difficult to understand ~ the language barriers are significant, and he doesn’t seem to be speaking from experience but from intellectual conviction, which leaves an unpalatable tone of #opinion.

He talks about the Buddhist view that all 'knowledge and perception' is 'inherently delusive', and about a 'degenerative philosophy of history' that characterises some '"higher" forms of Buddhism'. This degenerative view (the yuga system) seems to have been Hindu, pre-Buddhist, and as far as I know was not held by the Buddha. It was picked up again later by 'Buddhists', who merged Buddhism with belief systems already extant in their culture. 

That said, there are suttas about the devolution and evolution of beings  ([[DN 26 Cakkavatti Sutta, 'The Wheel-Turning Monarch'|DN 26]] [[DN 27 Aggaññasutta, 'What Came First|DN 27]], and [[DN 1 Brahmajāla Sutta, 'The Divine Net'|DN 1]]). However the Buddhists or Hindus viewed this, it seems like one of those metaphysical claims we can never be sure of, anymore than we can be sure of the Big Bag. And I suppose the question is how does one's faith in this impact their practice?
