#self #anattā #non-self #metaphysics 

A portrayal of the Way between [[metaphysics#The Way between Two Extremes|the extremes of eternalism and annihilationism]], where there is space to accept the ambiguity around the existence of a soul. I don't need to believe there is nothing after one sees through the illusion of self, or hold a robust metaphysical position about #self other than it is a process. In fact, in [MN 72](https://www.dhammatalks.org/suttas/MN/MN72.html), the Buddha advises strongly that holding any position on such things as the existence of a soul causes 

> suffering, distress, despair, & fever, 

and, anyway 
 
 > does not lead to disenchantment, dispassion, cessation; to calm, direct knowledge, self-awakening, unbinding

The Buddha didn't make absolute metaphysical claims/statements about the [[07. What the Buddha Taught, and Didn't Teach#The Ten Unprofitable Questions|unprofitable questions]] of MN 72, and I can't gain certainty about these questions through science *or* Buddhism - only through experience can I gain direct insight into the metaphysical questions that do actually matter, such as [[07. What the Buddha Taught, and Didn't Teach#There is a Cause for Suffering|what is the cause of suffering]]?
