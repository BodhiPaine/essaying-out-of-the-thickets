There are obviously many features of the Buddhist worldview that seem alien to the average Western materialist, especially the cosmology and mythology: in the Marks of a Great Man, the Buddha is said to have ==webbed feet== and ==eyes in his hands==, etc. When Buddha *descended from the Tusita heaven* the ==devas caused an earthquake==, etc. And there is an increasing tendency to take a lot of this stuff with a grain of salt, chalking it up as Secular Buddhism. I get it, but I want to make sure I am not throwing the baby out with the bathwater. 

It may be, as one respected teacher suggested, that all the fantastical mythology and cosmology is found in suttas that don't deal with the pragmatic aspects of the teaching, so the method can be taken separately from the full-blown mythological aspects of the suttas. This is a view I will hold lightly and consider, as the view of a respected teacher, but I will obviously need to explore this myself. ==The Net of Views==, for example, seems to be borderline - holding certain views impacts our practice. I guess it will be a matter of separating the pragmatic from the purely mythological, straddling the line between [[05. Tripping Over Worldviews#Psychological and Literal Interpretations of Cosmology|psychological and literal]] interpretations. 
# Multiverse
Belief in a multiverse of ever-evolving consciousness is a choice that feels fictional because it is [[metaphysics|metaphysical]], but such a view might be supported by Sheldrake's morphic-resonance theory, and also Buddha taught that we devolved from light beings ([[DN 27 Aggaññasutta, 'What Came First|DN 27]]), and are evolving back into light beings ([[DN 1 Brahmajāla Sutta, 'The Divine Net'|DN 1]]).[^1]
# The Hindu Yuga System
[[Capriles]] describes the Buddhist view of a 'degenerative philosophy of history' that characterises some '"higher" forms of Buddhism' and claims it is *the* Buddhist view, but as far as I know the Buddha didn't talk much about it. It was picked up again later and elaborated by Buddhists when Buddhism merged with other belief systems already extant in their culture.

That said, there are suttas about the devolution ([[DN 27 Aggaññasutta, 'What Came First|DN 27]]) and evolution of beings ([[DN 1 Brahmajāla Sutta, 'The Divine Net'|DN 1]]), including in [[DN 26 Cakkavatti Sutta, 'The Wheel-Turning Monarch'|DN 26]] where the king neglects the people and their lifespan reduces to 10 years. There is a general reduction in skilfulness and increase in suffering, before the cycle returns and beings evolve to lifespans lasting 80,000 years. So the Buddha may have held such a cosmological view, and I suppose the question is how does one's faith in this [[00.01. Faith and Effort|impact their practice]]?#SkillfulEffort
