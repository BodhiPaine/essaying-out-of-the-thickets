# About 'Me'
As good a place to start as any is with 心, which is more 'me' than anything else. I checked in with the idea for a writing prompt, and found this [nice but anonymous post](https://lalala-nihon.com/blog-articles/kokoro) that surveys the very broad and deep relevance of this humble[^1] little character that is burdened with [[the unbearable lightness]] %%link this to a heading in Reading List%% of representing nothing less than 'the essence of one's being'. 

The character means *kokoro*, and in Japanese culture it stretches back thousands of years and connects, unsurprisingly, with Buddhism. (The chronology here might be a bit spurious, because I don't think Buddhism had met Japan thousands of years ago ~ it had to travel there from ancient India through China over at least hundreds of years, so it might be more accurate to place it's connection with Buddhism at around a thousand years ago, but caveat: that's a half-informed guess.) But whenever, and since then, it has come to span so many domains of the human condition that I might say it has a laundry list of applications. 

Now, 心 has a place at the heart of Japanese literature, poetry, art, philosophy, science, psychology, spirituality, scripture, daily life ... and even the Bushido Way of the Samurai! It means something like a combination of 'body', 'mind', 'heart', and 'spirit', and for that reason it is more 'me' than anything else I am currently aware of, and more 'you' or even 'human' than the regular self-obsessed iteration we seem to be currently stuck in. Considered to be the seat of both emotions and awareness, a well-lived life depends, fundamentally, on cultivating a tranquil 心 through the application of quite-specific discipline which, lucky for us, Siddhartha Gotama got a handle on before he spent the next 45 years as the peripatetic pedagogue[^2] *par excellence*. 

Picking terms out of that anonymous Japanse-language-school post now, the idea and experience of 心 is intimately associated with such beautiful things as #consciousness and #morality, #sincerity, #intuition, and 'internal struggle'. In Zen, a tranquil and balanced 心 is essential for #enlightenment and at the development of its full potential might[^3] even be synonymous with that seemingly elusive state of #liberation from #suffering. Practitioners of #dhamma endeavour to cultivate harmony between the #intellect and our #emotions through #meditation intended to quell the turmoil of a constantly distracted and restless 心. A strong and stable 心 is essential for a samurai to remain calm in battle or when they are scaling skyscrapers to visit their Salarymen brethren for a tea ceremony in the spirit of #honour, #courage, and #loyalty. Beautiful, no ~ such that I probably shouldn't crack jokes about the Salarymen culture, which is unbearable tragic even just on the surface. But I haven't even finished yet ~ time for a push to GitHub and the beginning of a new paragraph. 

The concept of 心 has been embraced in the mystical arts of #neuroscience and #psychology, presumably both transpersonal and clinical and, 🤞🏼, the dreaded #psychiatry. (I like #hashtags, have you noticed ~ some of these might get replaced with links one day, and the #tags redirected to the opening or closing metadata, 🤷. I also really like #emojis, and #punctuation if you'd like to know about 'me' as well as about 心.) According to my sole resource at the moment, that one blog post promoting a language school in Japan, researchers in these disciplines are exploring 心 as a way of understanding the #mind-body connection that Descarte famously denied and left us all writhing in a literalist apoplexy that is now coming to an end with the death of [[metaphysical materialism]] and [[late-stage industrial capitalism]]. So that's cool! No more extractive stupidity and a lot more #holism.

Time for a pivot toward something more uplifting than the Sixth Great Extinction, not to be confused with the Great Depression though of course there are metaphorical correlates: 心 was explored in the 1914 novel *Kokoro* by Natsume Sōseki ~ missed opportunity right there, not using the symbol for the title. I was dimly aware of this novel, but it has gone straight to the top of the [[Reading List]] after *Lacuna* because I have been really looking forward to reading that, hoping it is light-hearted evening reading that aligns enough with the dhamma to not be a transgression of the no-entertainment precept: despite appearances such as this heavy-lifting About page, I am not actually constantly being a massive drag-weight by thinking about philosophy all the time ~ I do have a need to 'Netflix and chill' sometimes, but I try to not hijack the ol' dopamine pumps too much anymore these days, so a bit of light poetic prose is really my jam.[^4]
